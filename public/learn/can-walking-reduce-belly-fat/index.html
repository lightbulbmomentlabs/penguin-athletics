Can walking reduce belly fat?

By the Penguin Athletics editorial team Published November 21, 2026 6 min read

The short answer

Yes, a little. In trials where walking was the only change, waist size shrank by about 1.5 cm on average, and reviews show regular aerobic exercise like brisk walking can reduce the deep fat around your organs, sometimes without much weight loss. You can't target belly fat with ab exercises, so overall activity and eating habits matter more than any single move.

"Belly fat" is one of the most searched phrases in fitness, and walking is one of the easiest things you can do about it. So does it actually work?

The short version: walking can help, but not the way most ads promise. It won't melt your middle in two weeks, and it can't pick where your body loses fat. What it can do is chip away at the fat that matters most for your health, sometimes even when your weight stays about the same.

Here's what the research shows, why the type of belly fat matters, and how to walk in a way that gives you the best shot. (For the bigger picture, see our guide to walking for weight loss.)

Why does belly fat matter for health?

Not all belly fat is the same. Some sits just under your skin. Some, called visceral fat, sits deeper, packed around your organs.

That deeper fat is the one researchers care about most. A 2019 position statement from two international expert groups says visceral fat is "an independent risk marker of cardiovascular and metabolic morbidity and mortality" [1]. In plain English, more of it is linked to a higher risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes, even apart from body weight [1].

The U.S. National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) says that when most of your body fat sits around your waist rather than your hips, your risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes is higher [2]. It notes a higher risk with a waist of more than 35 inches for women or more than 40 inches for men [2].

That's why this article is about health, not looks. A smaller waist can be a sign that the fat around your organs is shrinking.

Does walking actually shrink your waist?

Yes, a little. The best walking-specific evidence comes from a review of 32 randomized trial reports in inactive adults, where walking was the only change [3]. Compared with people who didn't exercise, walkers saw [3]:

  • Waist size down about 1.51 cm (a bit over half an inch)
  • Body fat down about 1.22 percentage points
  • Weight down about 1.37 kg (roughly 3 pounds)

Those are modest numbers. But the same trials also found better fitness and lower blood pressure [3], so the waist change wasn't the only payoff.

Smaller trials that scanned the belly directly point the same way. In one 12-week trial, 20 women with higher weight were randomly assigned to walk or stay inactive [4]. The walkers went three days a week for 50 to 70 minutes at a moderate pace [4]. They had significant drops in both the fat under the skin of the belly and the deeper visceral fat, while the inactive group didn't change [4]. That's a small study, so treat it as supporting evidence, not proof on its own.

Can walking reduce belly fat without weight loss?

This is the encouraging part. Aerobic exercise seems to work on visceral fat even when the scale barely moves.

A meta-analysis pooled 15 studies with 852 people with higher weight who did aerobic or other exercise without cutting calories [5]. Exercise reduced visceral fat, and moderate- or high-intensity aerobic training worked best [5]. The authors reported reductions of more than 30 square centimeters in women and more than 40 in men on CT scans, seen in as little as 12 weeks [5].

A well-known trial in men tested this head-on [6]. One group exercised without losing weight [6]. Their weight didn't change over 3 months, yet their belly fat and visceral fat still went down [6]. The authors concluded that "exercise without weight loss reduces abdominal fat" [6].

Those studies looked at aerobic exercise in general, which includes brisk walking but isn't limited to it. Still, it's a good reason not to judge your walks by the scale alone.

Do ab exercises burn belly fat faster?

No. The idea that working one body part burns the fat on top of it is called spot reduction, and the research doesn't support it.

In one trial, people did seven ab exercises, five days a week, for six weeks [7]. Their ab endurance improved, but belly fat, waist size, and body fat didn't change compared with a control group [7].

A 2022 meta-analysis of 13 studies with 1,158 people looked at training one limb while leaving the other untrained [8]. Fat loss was no different between the trained and untrained side [8]. The authors' conclusion was blunt: "there was no spot reduction" [8].

Ab exercises are still good for strength and core control. They just aren't a shortcut for belly fat. Whole-body activity like walking is the better tool.

How much walking does it take?

More activity generally means more change. A 2023 meta-analysis of 40 randomized trials with 2,190 people found that exercise reduced visceral fat, and the effect grew as the weekly amount of exercise went up [9]. Cutting calories also reduced visceral fat in that review [9].

Intensity may matter too. A review of 25 trials with 1,686 people found that regular aerobic exercise trimmed waist size by about 3.2 cm compared with no exercise [10]. Vigorous exercise cut waist size by about 4.2 cm, compared with about 2.5 cm for moderate exercise [10]. The authors said higher intensity "may offer superior benefit" [10].

So the takeaway is simple: walk regularly, build up your time, and pick up the pace when you can. For step-based targets, see how many steps a day to lose weight?

How to walk for a healthier waistline

You don't need a fancy plan. Try this:

  1. Start where you are. If you're not active now, begin with short walks most days. Our guide to starting when you hate exercise can help.
  2. Build up your weekly minutes. In the research, more exercise led to more visceral fat loss [9]. Add a few minutes each week.
  3. Go brisk. Moderate- to high-intensity aerobic exercise worked best for visceral fat [5]. Aim for a pace where talking is possible but singing isn't.
  4. Add faster stretches when you're ready. Vigorous exercise cut waist size more than moderate exercise [10]. Short bursts of faster walking are an easy way to try this.
  5. Give it at least 12 weeks. Many of the trials that found changes ran about three months [4][5][6].
  6. Measure your waist, not just your weight. The NHLBI suggests measuring just above your hip bones, right after you breathe out [2]. Check once a month, not every day.
  7. Pair it with eating changes if you want more. Cutting calories also reduced visceral fat in trials [9]. A doctor or registered dietitian can help you find a safe approach.

When should you talk to your doctor?

Walking is safe for most people. Check with your doctor before starting a new routine if any of these apply:

  • You have heart disease, high blood pressure, or diabetes.
  • You take medicine that affects your blood sugar or blood pressure.
  • You have joint pain, an injury, or a higher risk of falls.
  • You get chest pain, dizziness, or unusual shortness of breath when you're active.
  • You have a history of an eating disorder, or focusing on your body or weight affects your mental health.

FAQ

How long until walking changes my waist?

Many trials saw changes in visceral fat or waist size after about 12 weeks of regular exercise [4][5]. Changes are usually gradual.

Is walking or running better for belly fat?

Vigorous exercise cut waist size a bit more than moderate exercise in one review [10]. But the best choice is the one you'll actually keep doing.

Can I lose belly fat by walking alone?

You can lose some. Walking-only trials trimmed waist size by about 1.5 cm on average [3]. Eating changes can add to that [9]. See can you lose weight just by walking?

Why is my waist shrinking but not my weight?

Exercise can reduce belly fat even when body weight stays the same [6]. That's a real health win, so keep going.

  • How many steps a day to lose weight?

    Weight · 6 min readThere's no single step count that guarantees weight loss. In a 2026 review of weight-loss trials, people who raised their steps to about 8,500 a day and stayed there kept more weight off, but the extra steps didn't speed up the initial loss. Walking on its own usually leads to modest weight loss, so steps work best alongside eating changes.

  • How many calories does walking burn?

    Weight · 6 min readA 30-minute brisk walk uses roughly 140 to 240 calories for most adults, and in one study, walking a mile used about 90 to 110 calories. The biggest factors are your body weight, your pace, and whether you're going uphill. Every calorie number, including the one on your watch, is an estimate, so treat it as a rough guide.
