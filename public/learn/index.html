  • What is a good daily step goal?

    Basics · 6 min readThere's no single magic number. Large studies link about 7,000 steps a day to much lower health risks than 2,000, with benefits leveling off around 6,000 to 8,000 steps for adults 60 and older and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults. The best personal goal starts from your current daily average and climbs a little at a time.

  • Walking for weight loss: the complete guide

    Weight · 11 min readWalking on its own usually leads to modest weight loss. In a review of pedometer-based walking programs with no diet changes, people lost about 1.3 kg (roughly 3 pounds) over a median of 16 weeks. Walking does more when paired with changes in eating, and it helps prevent weight gain and improves blood sugar and insulin sensitivity whatever the scale says.

  • Walking and your mind: the complete guide

    Mind · 10 min readWalking is good for your mental health. A 2024 review of 75 trials found that walking reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety in adults, and people who move more tend to have a lower risk of depression. Walking works best alongside therapy or medication when you need them, not instead of them.

  • Walking and longevity: the complete guide

    Longevity · 11 min readPeople who walk more tend to live longer. In a 2025 review of 57 studies, 7,000 steps a day was linked to a 47% lower risk of early death compared with 2,000 steps, and the benefits started to level off around 5,000 to 7,000 steps. Most of this evidence is observational, so it shows a strong link rather than proof, but walking is also linked to lower dementia risk and better function as you age.

  • Walking and your heart: the complete guide

    Heart · 11 min readWalking is one of the best-studied ways to help your heart. In trials it lowered systolic blood pressure by about 4 mmHg, and in long-term studies people who walked the most had about 31% lower risk of cardiovascular disease than people who walked the least. Aim for about 150 minutes a week at a brisk pace, and start smaller if you need to.

  • Walking basics: the complete beginner's guide

    Basics · 12 min readAdults need at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and brisk walking counts. That's about 30 minutes a day, five days a week, at a pace where you can talk but not sing. If you're starting from zero, any amount helps, so begin small and build up gradually.

  • How to start walking when you hate exercise

    Basics · 7 min readStart smaller than you think you need to, like a 5 to 10 minute walk tied to something you already do every day, and add a few minutes each week. Health guidelines say some activity is better than none and short walks count. New habits took about 66 days on average to feel automatic in one study, so aim for consistency, not intensity.

  • How many steps a day to live longer?

    Longevity · 7 min readIn a pooled analysis of 15 studies, the risk of dying early kept falling as steps rose, then leveled off at about 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day for adults 60 and older and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults. You don't need 10,000 to benefit: a 2025 review linked 7,000 steps a day to a 47% lower risk of death than 2,000 steps.

  • How many steps a day do you need for heart health?

    Heart · 6 min readYou probably don't need 10,000. In large studies, people who took more steps had a lower risk of heart disease, with most of the benefit showing up by about 7,000 to 9,000 steps a day. In adults 60 and older, 6,000 to 9,000 steps a day was linked to a 40% to 50% lower risk of heart disease than about 2,000 steps.

  • Does walking lower blood pressure?

    Heart · 6 min readYes. A large Cochrane review of 73 trials found that regular walking lowered systolic blood pressure (the top number) by about 4 mmHg on average. Most of the walking programs in those trials averaged around 150 minutes of moderate walking a week for a few months.

  • Does walking help anxiety?

    Mind · 7 min readYes, for many people it helps. A 2024 review of 26 trials in adults found that regular walking reduced anxiety symptoms compared with doing nothing, and a single bout of exercise can bring a small short-term drop too. Walking works best alongside therapy or medication when you need them, not instead of them.

  • Can you lose weight just by walking?

    Weight · 6 min readYes, but usually only a little. In trials of walking alone, people lost about 1.4 kg (roughly 3 pounds) on average, and programs that combine walking with eating changes lead to more weight loss. Walking still improves blood pressure, blood sugar, and fitness even when the scale barely moves.
