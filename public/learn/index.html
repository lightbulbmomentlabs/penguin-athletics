  • How to walk with good posture

    Basics · 6 min readStand tall, keep your chin level and your eyes about 10 to 20 feet ahead, relax your shoulders down and back, let your arms swing naturally, and roll smoothly from heel to toe without overstriding. Letting your arms swing matters: holding them still took about 12% more energy in one lab study. There's no single perfect posture, so aim for comfortable and relaxed, not rigid.

  • Walking speed and life expectancy: what's the link?

    Longevity · 6 min readPeople who naturally walk faster tend to live longer. In a pooled study of 34,485 adults 65 and older, each 0.1 meter per second of extra walking speed was linked to a 12% lower risk of death, and speeds of about 1.0 meter per second (around 2.2 mph) or faster went with longer-than-expected survival. Speed is partly a sign of overall health, so it shows a strong link rather than proof that walking faster adds years.

  • Does walking after meals lower blood sugar?

    Weight · 6 min readYes. Trials show that walking soon after you eat lowers the blood sugar rise that follows a meal. In one trial of adults with type 2 diabetes, a 10-minute walk after each main meal lowered after-meal blood sugar about 12% more than a single daily 30-minute walk, and about 22% more after dinner.

  • Does walking lower cholesterol?

    Heart · 5 min readA little, but the evidence is mixed. A review of 25 trials found regular walking lowered LDL ("bad") cholesterol by about 5%, while a later review of 32 walking trials found no clear change in blood fats. Walking helps your heart in other proven ways, but on its own it usually won't fix high cholesterol.

  • How does walking reduce stress?

    Mind · 6 min readWalking seems to ease stress in a few ways. Regular physical activity reduces psychological distress in trials, and active people tend to have a healthier daily cortisol rhythm. Time outdoors adds to it: in one study, 20 to 30 minutes in nature gave the biggest drop in the stress hormone cortisol for the time spent.

  • What's the best time of day to walk?

    Basics · 6 min readThere's no single best time. Large studies disagree, linking morning, midday, and evening activity to lower risks, and walking at any time of day is linked to a longer life. If you want a tiebreaker, a 10-minute walk after each meal lowered after-meal blood sugar about 12% more than one daily 30-minute walk in a trial of adults with type 2 diabetes.

  • How many steps a day to lose weight?

    Weight · 6 min readThere's no single step count that guarantees weight loss. In a 2026 review of weight-loss trials, people who raised their steps to about 8,500 a day and stayed there kept more weight off, but the extra steps didn't speed up the initial loss. Walking on its own usually leads to modest weight loss, so steps work best alongside eating changes.

  • Walking vs running: which is better for your heart?

    Heart · 6 min readBoth are good for your heart, and neither clearly wins. In a study of about 49,000 walkers and runners, burning the same amount of energy by walking or running led to similar drops in the risk of high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, and possibly heart disease. Running gets you there in less time, while walking takes longer but is easier to start and stick with.

  • Is walking good for depression?

    Mind · 7 min readYes, walking can help. A 2024 review of 44 trials in adults found walking reduced depression symptoms compared with not walking, and the effect was bigger in people who were already depressed. Walking works best as part of your care, alongside therapy or medication when you need them, not as a replacement.

  • What is a good daily step goal?

    Basics · 6 min readThere's no single magic number. Large studies link about 7,000 steps a day to much lower health risks than 2,000, with benefits leveling off around 6,000 to 8,000 steps for adults 60 and older and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults. The best personal goal starts from your current daily average and climbs a little at a time.

  • Is the 10,000 steps rule real?

    Longevity · 6 min readThe 10,000 steps rule is not based on science. It traces back to a marketing campaign in Japan. Large studies find health benefits start at as few as about 2,500 steps a day, with the drop in risk of early death leveling off at around 7,000 to 9,000 steps for most adults, though 10,000 is still a fine goal if you can reach it.

  • How many calories does walking burn?

    Weight · 6 min readA 30-minute brisk walk uses roughly 140 to 240 calories for most adults, and in one study, walking a mile used about 90 to 110 calories. The biggest factors are your body weight, your pace, and whether you're going uphill. Every calorie number, including the one on your watch, is an estimate, so treat it as a rough guide.
