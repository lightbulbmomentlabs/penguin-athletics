  • Is brisk walking enough cardio?

    Heart · 5 min readYes, for most people brisk walking is real cardio. The CDC counts walking at 2.5 miles per hour or faster as moderate-intensity activity, and 150 minutes a week of it meets the adult guideline. In trials, regular walking raised fitness and lowered blood pressure, so you don't need to run to get heart benefits.

  • Hot girl walks: does the trend actually help your mood?

    Mind · 6 min readProbably, yes, though no one has studied the hot girl walk by name. Walks as short as 10 to 15 minutes reliably lift mood in experiments, and a 2024 review of 75 trials found regular walking reduced depression and anxiety symptoms in adults. The gratitude and playlist parts may add a little, but the walking does most of the work.

  • Do you need special walking shoes?

    Basics · 6 min readNo. For everyday walking, the NHS says any shoes or trainers that are comfortable, supportive, and don't cause blisters will do. Fit matters more than the label: studies suggest 63% to 72% of people wear shoes that don't match the length or width of their feet, and poor fit is linked to foot pain. Choose a pair that fits well and feels good, and save specialist walking shoes for long or rough routes.

  • Is it too late to start walking at 60?

    Longevity · 6 min readNo, it isn't too late. Large studies link becoming more active in midlife and later to a lower risk of early death, and a review of adults 60 and older linked even small amounts of moderate activity to a 22% lower risk. In a trial of adults aged 70 to 89, a walking-based program lowered the risk of losing the ability to walk about a quarter mile.
