The hot girl walk took over TikTok with a simple idea: go for a long walk, play something good, and spend the time thinking about what you're grateful for, what you want, and how great you are. Plenty of people swear it changed their mood.

Nobody has run a study on the hot girl walk by name. But each piece of it has been studied: the walking, the gratitude, the music, the time outside, even the walk with friends. Here's what that research says, and which parts are doing the heavy lifting. (For the bigger picture, see our guide to [walking and your mind](/learn/walking-mental-health/).)

## Does a walk actually lift your mood?

Yes, and fast. In four experiments, researchers had people walk for just 10 to 15 minutes [1]. Walking consistently shifted people toward feeling more energized and more pleasant, and they settled into calm and relaxation afterward [1]. That pattern held both outdoors and in the lab, and across different groups of people [1].

A later set of three experiments found that walking lifted mood even when people had no idea the study was about mood [2]. It even worked when the walk was boring, and when people were told to expect something unpleasant [2]. In other words, you don't have to believe in it for it to work.

Studies in people your age point the same way. In one small trial with young adults (average age 21), a 10-minute brisk walk cut feelings of fatigue compared with sitting, though its effect on overall mood scores was less clear [3]. The CDC also notes that some brain benefits of physical activity happen right after a session of moderate-to-vigorous activity [4].

## Does walking regularly help more?

This is where the bigger benefits seem to be. A 2024 review pooled 75 randomized trials with 8,636 people [5]. Among adults, walking programs reduced depression symptoms and anxiety symptoms compared with people who didn't walk [5].

Those benefits showed up whether people walked indoors or outdoors, alone or in a group, and at different frequencies and durations [5]. People who were already depressed saw bigger improvements than people who weren't, but both groups benefited [5]. Walking also worked about as well as the other active approaches it was compared with [5].

So one hot girl walk can brighten your afternoon. A habit of them is what the research on longer-term mood is built on. If you want to go deeper, see our articles on [walking and depression](/learn/is-walking-good-for-depression/) and [walking and anxiety](/learn/does-walking-help-anxiety/).

## Does the gratitude part do anything?

A little. A 2021 meta-analysis pooled 27 studies with 3,675 people and looked at gratitude exercises, like listing things you're thankful for [6]. Gratitude exercises had a small effect on depression and anxiety symptoms, both right after and at follow-up [6].

The effect was bigger when gratitude was compared with doing nothing than when it was compared with another activity [6]. The authors called the effects "relatively modest" and suggested people with depression or anxiety lean on approaches with stronger evidence [6].

That doesn't make the gratitude part pointless. It's free, it can't hurt, and it gives your brain something kinder to chew on than your inbox. Just know the walking is likely doing more of the work than the affirmations.

## Does music make a walk feel better?

It seems to. A large 2020 review combined 139 studies on music during exercise and sport, with 3,599 people in total [7]. Listening to music was linked to more positive feelings during activity, and it made the effort feel a bit easier [7].

The authors concluded that music can help people feel better during many kinds of physical activity [7]. So yes, the playlist matters. Pick songs that make you want to move.

## Is it better to walk outside?

Often, yes, though any walk counts. In a study of 35 active young women (average age 22), both a 10-minute outdoor walk and a 10-minute indoor walk improved how they felt [8]. But the outdoor walk felt more pleasant, was more enjoyable, and made them more likely to want to walk again [8].

Green space may add something extra. In one experiment, healthy adults who took a 90-minute walk in a natural area ruminated less afterward, meaning they spent less time stuck replaying negative thoughts [9]. People who walked for 90 minutes in an urban setting didn't get that benefit [9].

Still, the 2024 review found indoor walking helped mood symptoms too [5]. A walking pad or a mall loop on a rainy day is still a real walk.

## Is a hot girl walk with friends just as good?

It can be. A review of 42 studies with 1,843 people looked at outdoor walking groups [10]. People in walking groups had lower depression scores by the end, along with improvements in blood pressure and fitness [10]. No notable side effects were reported [10].

The original trend is a solo walk, and alone time has its place. But if a friend makes you more likely to actually go, bring them along. The same 2024 review found both group and solo walking reduced mood symptoms [5].

## What about feeling "hot"?

Here's a quieter benefit. A meta-analysis of 57 exercise programs found that exercise led to a small improvement in body image compared with not exercising [11]. How often people exercised made a difference [11].

That fits the spirit of the trend. The point isn't changing how you look. It's feeling more at home in your body, and regular movement seems to help with that.

## How to try a hot girl walk that actually helps

1. **Start with 10 to 15 minutes.** That's enough to lift your mood in experiments [1][3]. Go longer when you have time.
2. **Make it a habit, not a one-off.** Regular walking is where the bigger mood benefits show up [5]. A few walks a week is a solid start.
3. **Build a playlist you love.** Upbeat songs can make the walk feel better and easier [7].
4. **Head outside if you can.** A park, a waterfront, or a tree-lined street is a bonus [8][9].
5. **Pick one or two things to be grateful for.** Keep it light. It's a small boost, not homework [6].
6. **Invite a friend sometimes.** Walking together counts too [10].
7. **Track your streak.** Seeing your walks add up makes it easier to keep going on low-energy days.

New to walking, or not a fan of exercise? Our guide on [how to start walking when you hate exercise](/learn/how-to-start-walking-when-you-hate-exercise/) can help. And if stress is the main thing you're walking off, read [how walking reduces stress](/learn/how-walking-reduces-stress/).

## When a walk isn't enough

A good walk can lift a bad day. It can't replace care when you need it.

If low mood, anxiety, or stress lasts for weeks or gets in the way of work, sleep, or relationships, talk to a doctor or mental health professional. If you take medication for your mood, keep taking it as prescribed, and talk to your doctor before changing anything. Walking works well alongside treatment, not instead of it.

If you or someone you know is in crisis, call or text **988** to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in the US. It's free, confidential, and available 24/7 [12]. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

## FAQ

### How long should a hot girl walk be?

There's no magic distance. Walks of 10 to 15 minutes lifted mood in experiments, so start there and go longer if you like [1].

### Does the hot girl walk help with weight loss?

It's not designed for that, and this article focuses on mood. Walking supports health in lots of ways, but for weight, see our [walking for weight loss](/learn/walking-weight-loss/) guide.

### Can I do a hot girl walk on a treadmill?

Yes. Indoor walking reduced depression and anxiety symptoms in trials, though outdoor walks may feel a bit more enjoyable [5][8].

### Do I have to think positive thoughts?

No. Walking lifted mood even when people weren't trying to feel better [2]. Gratitude is a nice extra, not a requirement.
